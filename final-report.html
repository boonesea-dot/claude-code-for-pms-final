Release 4.2 quietly starved four responders. Here is how, and how we stop it happening again.

Two changes shipped on 12 August. Pings started going unanswered, a scoring rule turned each miss into a penalty that could not wear off, and four responders stopped hearing from us at all. This is the evidence, the fix I prototyped, and the checklist I now run on every brief.

What happened

4.2 shipped two changes at once. The shorter wait made misses common. The scoring rule treats a miss as a refusal and never forgives it. Together they trap a responder at the bottom of the list.

The loop, as the code works today

14.2 shipsPing wait cut from 90s to 60s. Proximity weight raised from 0.45 to 0.60.
2More missesA ping not answered in 60s counts as missed. Misses go from 2.3% to 18.0% of pings.
3Score dropsA miss costs 0.12, the same as a turn-down. A yes earns back only 0.08.
4Rank fallsRecent acceptance now carries less (0.25) and proximity more (0.60), so neighbours win.
5Fewer pingsThe score only changes when a responder is pinged. No pings, no chance to climb.
6StuckNothing fades the score back. In the code, a responder who sinks stays there.
Step 6 feeds back into step 5. Nothing the responder or their handler does can break it.

What this does and doesn't prove.
  • The data shows what changed and when. The mechanism above is a hypothesis built from the code and a replay.
  • The wait and the weights shipped together, so we can't yet say which caused what. A one-change-at-a-time replay of 12–14 Aug is still to do (Marcus).
  • A replay of every ping through the code's rules puts all four at 0.00 by 6 Sep. That is modelled. The live scores are owner-reported as near zero and not yet confirmed by Wen Li and Marcus.
  • "It's seasonal" can't be tested: the database starts on 29 Jun 2026, so there is no prior August to compare. The step on release day, and the tickets that start the same week, argue against it.
  • Callouts per day also fell 15% for an unknown reason, so raw pings-taken counts overstate 4.2's effect. The miss rate is the better measure.

Module4X-ray vision

The score only goes down, and a miss costs more than a yes earns

Routing code

Finding: Each responder has one score from 0 to 1. A yes adds 0.08. A turn-down or a miss takes off 0.12. Nothing fades, resets or adjusts it, and it only changes when they are pinged. So anyone who takes fewer than about 60% of their pings drifts to zero, and a low-ranked responder needs about 12 yeses in a row to climb back from 0.

A yes+0.08
A miss or no−0.12
The prompt I wrote
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.

00-rook/starved-responders-hypothesis.md, which also notes the code and production differ (production pings neighbouring areas first) and that the README omits the miss-lowers-score loop and the Supply dependency.

The fix, through the prototype

Scores ease back toward a neutral 0.5 on their own, a responder with no ping in N days shows as quiet on the console (N starts at 7, subject to change), and an admin can reset a score to 0.5 once so the four don't have to wait. It is written as what we would do if we're right.

Try it

  1. In the prototype, switch The fix from Off to On and drag Days since the fix shipped. Farlight's score climbs without a single ping.
  2. Slide Flag as quiet after N days to see when the console flags her. It changes the flag, not the recovery speed.
  3. Switch to Responder phone (Farlight), simulate a ping, and take it or turn it down. The result flows back to the console score and chart.
  4. Use the Admin tool to reset her to 0.50. With "keeps missing" on, her score slides back toward zero: a reset alone won't hold.
  5. Press Reset demo to start again.

Scores in the prototype are modelled. Only Farlight's last ping (28 Aug) is real; the other responders are sample data.

What the fix deliberately doesn't do

It doesn't revert 4.2, change the 60s wait or the proximity weight (we can't separate them yet), promise anyone a ping, explain why these four miss about 4× as often (59% vs 14% for the other twelve), or touch the availability record Supply reads. Its biggest risk is stated in the brief: if they keep missing at 60 seconds, the fade only slows the slide. The brief sets a failure test for that.

How the skill keeps this from happening again

4.2 went out with unexamined calls. The review-checklist skill runs the same eight questions on any brief, every time, so the gaps show up before anything ships. It reviews what is written and never edits.
